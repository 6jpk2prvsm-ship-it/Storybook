# Review the story flow

Read with a child where possible. Let them choose; avoid explaining which option to pick. Note the approximate duration and where attention rises or drops. No names or other identifying details are needed in feedback.

## A short message to accompany the link

I'm testing a little interactive bedtime story. Could you try it on your phone or tablet, ideally reading it with a child, and send me a few honest thoughts? It has different routes, so there are no wrong choices. I'd love to know whether the choices feel interesting, the mystery makes sense and you'd want another story like it.

## Two useful routes

| Route | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| Longer investigation | Footprints → boathouse → scratches → shore → reeds → inlet → explanation → learn a knot |
| More direct investigation | Boathouse → test the oar → shore → inlet → explanation → watch for the otter |

Try the third ending, hearing another story, using **Previous scene** from an ending. Back is a deliberate navigation control; the story itself does not loop through the footprints indefinitely.

## What to check

- Can you read the text and select choices comfortably on your device?
- Does each choice lead to an interesting scene?
- Does the boat-and-oar explanation make sense whichever clues you followed?
- Do any sentences assume you visited somewhere you did not?
- Does the ending feel satisfying and settled enough for bedtime?
- Refresh halfway through: does your place return?
- Does **Previous scene** go back correctly, and **Start again** return to the beginning?

## Feedback to send back

1. Device/browser, and approximate reading duration.
2. Route or ending chosen.
3. Most enjoyable moment.
4. Any confusing moment (scene title or exact sentence helps).
5. Any choice that felt pointless or was hard to understand.
6. Would you want to read another? What would make you return?

This is a flow test. Pricing and willingness to pay can be explored after families have had a chance to use it more than once.
